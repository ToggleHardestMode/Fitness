## Scientific evidence and limits

1. **Calf position:** Kinoshita et al. (2023), 12 weeks, 14 untrained adults, within-person standing vs seated plantarflexion training, found larger lateral/medial gastrocnemius and whole-calf MRI volume gains standing, while soleus differences were not significant. This is **small-sample direct evidence** for a muscle-specific advantage, not a general superiority claim. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1272106/full
2. **Quadriceps specialization:** Kassiano et al. (2026) compared Smith-machine squats with leg extensions in 63 untrained young women and found greater rectus femoris hypertrophy after leg extensions but a greater distal vastus lateralis response after squats. This supports optional complementary selection, *not* replacing all squats. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41379528/
3. **Overall programming:** ACSM position stand (2026), an overview of 137 reviews in healthy adults, found resistance training effective, but no consistent superiority of equipment types across outcomes. It does **not** establish an exact ideal weekly set count for this owner. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/

## Experiment and training safeguards

- Keep V10.3 load-progression rule: only recommend load increases after **two comparable sessions at the top rep target, RPE ≤8, recovery score ≥4**. Do not interpret a missing recovery rating as 4.
- Set increases are **separate decisions**. First establish stable technique, tolerable effort, repeatable performance, adherence and recovery. After a stable baseline, trial only 1–2 extra direct weekly sets for one target muscle at a time; record what changed. Do not auto-increase at a fixed calendar date.
- Record exercise name+version, dumbbell kg **per dumbbell** or pulldown kg **per cable stack**, all working-set reps, RIR/RPE, actual rest, sleep/recovery, session time, and next-session performance.
- Lower body: record next-day and delayed symptoms; no exercise, readiness score, app import or calculation counts as PT clearance. Stop and seek professional review if symptoms worsen. New progressive neurological weakness, saddle numbness, or new bowel/bladder dysfunction need urgent medical evaluation.
- Unilateral exercise: **one prescribed set includes both sides** and is counted once. Core holds record seconds/side. Superset SS1 is optional; no need to force it.
- Versions are separate templates. Historical completed-set data must not be rewritten or auto-matched by exercise name. Linking equivalent historical exercises requires an owner-reviewed mapping, not silent merging.
