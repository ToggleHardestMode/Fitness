# V10.2 Home — overlap-aware programming and calculation notes

## Scope and important limits
- Baseline: actual `V10.1_Home_FitnessPilot_Import.csv`, 24 exercise entries, 62 sets (Mon 17 / Tue 13 / Thu 21 / Fri 11).
- V10.2 retains **exactly the same numbers of prescribed exercises and sets**. Only selected rowing emphasis, incline angles, and curl naming/cues change. This is a targeted refinement, **not a clinically validated new program**.
- You still report radiating leg symptoms, sometimes appearing the following day. Both lower-body days continue to require individual clinical screening before loaded progression. Nothing here certifies an exercise as medically safe.
- Session doses are *planned*. Effective-set equivalents are *estimates* and are not predictions of muscle growth, muscle gain in kg, or anatomical activation percentages. A patient's tolerance and actual completed sets must always take priority.

## Counting protocol
For every exercise, enter the sets once. Assign `1` to the chosen primary muscle region, `0.5` to a secondary muscle, and `0` when no meaningful hypertrophy-relevant contribution is assumed. A set may contribute to several muscle regions simultaneously; **do not add all region-equivalents into one grand total of physical sets**. Changing exercise technique may invalidate classifications.

Example: 4 flat DB bench sets = chest 4.0; front delts 2.0; triceps 2.0. This does NOT imply front delts gained half as much muscle as chest.

The 0.5 fractional method is supported as a better group-level research counting method than 0 or 1 for all indirect sets; individual exercise coefficients are transparent coaching estimates, not validated per-exercise conversion factors.

## Why these are the best current overlaps
1. **DB flat + low-incline + incline presses:** primarily chest, with front delts and triceps. 10 chest-direct sets already offer an efficient weekly growth benchmark. Use modest bench angles instead of repeating steep inclines.
2. **Chest-supported and bench-supported DB rows, elbows toward hips:** bias lats with additional work for biceps, upper back, rear delts, while limiting unsupported trunk loading. Eight lat-direct sets are a conservative but meaningful home-equipment dose.
3. **Chest-supported high rows, elbows out:** add a distinct upper-back/rear-delt emphasis missing from lat-biased rowing. Counts remain technique-dependent.
4. **DB lateral raises and reverse flies:** retain direct lateral and rear delt work because pressing does not fully replace it. Pressing already adds considerable front-delt stimulus, so avoid extra front raises.
5. **Two direct biceps and triceps variations:** keep four direct sets each despite ~9–10 set-equivalents, because rows/presses do not guarantee equal growth of every arm muscle or head. Incline curl is a tolerable candidate, not proven universally superior. The overhead DB triceps extension is optional if it irritates the shoulder/back; bench support is preferred.
6. **Do not force a dumbbell pullover as a pulldown replacement.** EMG studies have shown substantial pectoral activation and do not establish identical lat hypertrophy. A securely mounted pull-up bar or well-anchored band could improve vertical-pulling coverage later, if tolerated and mechanically safe.

## Exercise performance and the set model
- Only count a set as useful hypertrophy volume if it is completed with adequate control and reasonable effort. The Excel sheet models *planned* sets, not verified completed quality.
- Upper-body work: usually ~2–3 RIR, without provocation. Start lower-body programming only according to clinician guidance and symptom response.
- Review during, immediately after, next morning, and optionally at 48 h. If radiating pain/tingling/numbness worsens or moves farther down the leg, stop progressing the implicated movement and seek individual review; new weakness requires prompt assessment. New bladder/bowel symptoms or saddle numbness require emergency care.
- The older production app discards `source_note`; the V15.3 preview retains exercise instructions, PT review flags, and seconds-per-side holds. **Preserve the source guide, and never treat missing symptoms or app calculations as medical clearance.**
